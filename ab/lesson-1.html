Mini-Lesson (6 min): The “Average Rate” Tool

We’ll use this tool and then push it to its limit.

Core idea

Average rate of change from \\(t=a\\) to \\(t=b\\): \\[ \\frac{s(b)-s(a)}{b-a}. \\] Units: \\(\\frac{\\text{output units}}{\\text{input units}}\\).
If \\(b=a\\), then \\(b-a=0\\), so the average rate is undefined “at a point.”
That’s the problem calculus solves using the limit idea.

Quick Practice (students lead)

Do
A water tank has volume \\(V(t)\\) liters at time \\(t\\) minutes.
  1. What are the units of \\(\\frac{V(8)-V(2)}{8-2}\\)?
  2. If it equals \\(3\\), interpret it.
  3. How could you estimate the “rate at 5 minutes”?
Share: One person explains units, one explains interpretation, one explains “how to estimate at a time.”

Interactive Lab (18 min): “Instant” from “Near”

We model position by \\(s(t)=t^2\\) (meters). You’ll estimate the “instantaneous rate” at \\(t=t_0\\) by shrinking \\(h\\).
Model: s(t)=t² t₀: h:
Forward avg \\(\\frac{s(t_0+h)-s(t_0)}{h}\\): Symmetric avg \\(\\frac{s(t_0+h)-s(t_0-h)}{2h}\\):
Shrink h → look for stability

Student tasks (do these in order)

Tasks
  1. Pick a value of \\(t_0\\). Keep it fixed.
  2. Record the symmetric average rate for \\(h=1,0.5,0.2,0.1,0.05\\).
  3. Write a claim: “As \\(h\\to 0\\), the average rate approaches ____.”
  4. Explain what that number means in context (units + sentence).
AP habit: your conclusion must match your evidence (table).
