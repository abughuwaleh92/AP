Class 1 • Average Rate of Change (12–22 min)

Core idea

Average rate of change from \\(t=a\\) to \\(t=b\\) is \\[ \\frac{s(b)-s(a)}{b-a}. \\] Units: \\(\\text{(output units)}/\\text{(input units)}\\).
Important: At a single time instant (\\(b=a\\)), the fraction is undefined. So we can’t “plug in” a point for average rate.

Student task (8 min)

Solve
Let \\(s(t)\\) be position in meters, \\(t\\) in seconds.
  1. What are the units of \\(\\frac{s(b)-s(a)}{b-a}\\)?
  2. If average rate from 2 to 6 is 3, interpret it physically.
  3. How could we estimate the speed at 4 using averages?
Share 1 answer with class. Teacher collects 2–3 different interpretations.

Class 1 • Debrief (38–45 min)

Today’s “definition idea” (no heavy formality yet): \\[ \\text{Instantaneous rate at }t_0 \\approx \\text{values approached by average rates as the interval shrinks.} \\] In AP language: this becomes a limit next lesson.

Retrieval exit (2 min)

Answer quickly:
  1. What does \\(\\frac{s(b)-s(a)}{b-a}\\) represent?
  2. Why can’t we set \\(b=a\\) for “instant”?

Teacher quick close (5 min)

Class 2 • MCQ: Distractor Analysis (20–35 min)

MCQ (original)

A student estimates the instantaneous rate at \\(t=3\\) using: \\[ \\frac{s(3.2)-s(2.8)}{3.2-2.8}. \\] Which statement is most accurate?

Mini-debate protocol (15 min)

Debate
Step 1 (2 min): Choose an answer silently.
Step 2 (5 min): Pair up. Convince your partner using a clear “what does the quotient represent?” argument.
Step 3 (6 min): Two pairs merge (group of 4). If you disagree, identify which word in the answer choice is wrong.
Step 4 (2 min): Whole-class vote.
AP habit: identify what an expression means before calculating anything.

Class 2 • Mini-FRQ Write-up (35–42 min)

AP write-up checklist

Full-credit pattern:
  • Setup: show the symmetric quotient
  • Compute: correct arithmetic
  • Units: meters per second
  • Interpretation: one sentence in context
Teacher circulates and checks: students are stating what the expression represents.
