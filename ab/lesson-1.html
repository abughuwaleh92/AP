Average Rate of Change (The “Near” Tool)

Definition (in student language)

Average rate of change from \\(t=a\\) to \\(t=b\\) is \\[ \\frac{s(b)-s(a)}{b-a}. \\] It measures “how much per unit time” over an interval.
Key catch: if \\(b=a\\), then the denominator is 0, so the average rate of change is undefined at a single point. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}
So… how do we talk about change at an instant?
We look at average change over intervals that shrink around that instant. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

Student task (Think → Pair → Share)

Approaches
Teacher note: Today we’re building the idea that calculus uses limits to define instantaneous rate of change from average rates. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

So… what is “instantaneous rate”?

Instantaneous rate of change at \\(t=t_0\\) is defined using a limit of average rates: \\[ \\text{Instant rate at }t_0 \\;=\\; \\lim_{h\\to 0}\\frac{s(t_0+h)-s(t_0)}{h}. \\] This “limit idea” is what lets us talk about change at an instant. :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}
